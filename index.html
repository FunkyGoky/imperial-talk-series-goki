01

The Journey

From Leadership Bet to Customer Product

It Starts with a Vision, Not a Roadmap

  • Rufus was a top-down bet on LLMs — not a bottom-up product request
  • The PM's first job: translate the vision into a concrete problem statement
  • Amazon's mechanism: PR/FAQ forces you to write the customer experience before a line of code — even when leadership has already decided the direction
Key Insight: Writing the press release first forces clarity on who benefits and how — before engineering begins.

Build Narrow, Learn Fast

  • Rufus launched with a narrow scope deliberately — not because of lack of ambition, but strategic discipline
  • The #1 lesson: keep it narrow — don't try to solve everything at once
  • A/B testing drove major direction changes — the product that launched wasn't the product that was designed
Key Insight: A narrow launch is not a failure of ambition — it's the fastest path to learning what actually matters.

The UX and the Model Are One System

The biggest early failure: users didn't know what to ask Rufus. The fix wasn't to retrain the model — it was to redesign the UI.

UX Problem

Users faced a blank text box and didn't know where to start. Engagement was low.

UX Solution

Added suggested prompts — surfacing examples shaped user behaviour more than any model change.

The Lesson

In AI products, the interface shapes behaviour as much as the model does.

02

The 5 Walls Every AI Product Hits

What "Scale" Actually Means

"Most AI products don't die at launch. They die between launch and scale."

☕ The café-to-franchise analogy: Your café can be brilliant. Scale means the 500th coffee in a different city tastes the same — without you being there.

01

Technical Scale

Latency that holds at millions of requests, not hundreds

  • Rufus had to maintain response quality under real shopping traffic — people don't wait
  • Infrastructure must handle millions of concurrent requests without degrading response quality
  • Every additional 100ms of latency in e-commerce means measurable conversion loss

02

Evaluation at Scale

You can't manually review millions of outputs a day

  • Rufus used a combination of human quality raters, conversion signals, and latency metrics
  • Conversion lift after Rufus interactions became the north star metric — measurable, business-aligned
  • You need automated eval infrastructure before you need it — build it early
Hard Truth: At millions of queries per day, you can't rely on human review alone. Automated evaluation isn't optional — it's foundational.

03

Guardrails

The Constraint vs. Utility Tension

  • The hardest internal disagreement: how much to constrain what Rufus could say vs. letting it be genuinely useful
  • Too constrained → useless. Too open → brand risk, wrong answers, regulatory exposure
  • This tension never fully resolves — it's a continuous calibration
  • Real-world stakes: GDPR means what Rufus says about a product in Germany has different legal implications than in the US

04

Rollback & Resilience

When model quality drops in production

  • Rufus experienced a real model regression after an update — quality dropped in production
  • The rollback worked, but exposed a gap: evaluation infrastructure wasn't robust enough to catch it before users
  • The fix changed how the team thought about deployment gates
Key Lesson: Rollback for AI is harder than software — you're not just reverting code, you may be reverting learned behaviour.

05

Culture, Language & Market Expansion

Language ≠ Translation

  • Rufus launched US-first, then retrofitted other markets
  • "Retrofitting" sounds simple — it isn't. Shopping behaviour, query language, trust patterns, and return culture differ fundamentally across markets
  • What reads as helpful in the US can read as presumptuous in Japan
  • GDPR and regional data laws mean you can't just copy-paste the US architecture globally

03

Frequently Asked Questions

For Student Q&A

How do you know when an AI product is ready to ship?

There's no perfect — you define minimum thresholds, then the bar moves as you learn.

Who owns quality — PM, engineer, or data scientist?

All three. That's the real org design challenge.

What if the A/B test results are noisy?

You build confidence layers — conversion data, qualitative signals, human eval together.

How did Amazon balance speed with safety on Rufus?

Constrain scope early, expand carefully — narrow launch is not a failure of ambition.

What happens when the model gives wrong answers at scale?

Triage → rollback → root cause → fix evaluation gap → redeploy.

Does GDPR actually stop innovation?

It slows shortcuts, not innovation — good data design handles both.
